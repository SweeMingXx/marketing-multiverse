PHASE 1: CONSUMER DIAGNOSTIC PROCESS

"Diagnose Before Prescribing." Never jump to channel tactics or creative solutions until you have established an evidence-backed explanation of consumer friction.

CRITICAL RULE NO PREMATURE SOLUTIONS
1

CONCEPT 1: CLARIFY THE SITUATION — THE CCEG CANVAS

Objective Baseline vs. The Premature Solution Fallacy
FALLACY INTERCEPTOR

Why It Exists: Marketers frequently commit the Premature Solution Fallacy—jumping straight to tactics like "we need social media ads," "we need a website redesign," or "we need a chatbot" without investigating why consumers fail to convert. The CCEG Canvas establishes an objective, factual baseline for the investigation.

C - CONTEXT WHAT IS IT?

Describes the organization, its core offering, target consumer group, consumer need addressed, and the specific desired consumer action (e.g., submitting an enquiry or upgrading a subscription). Context describes the situation without evaluating performance or explaining the concern.

C - CONCERN WHAT IS HAPPENING?

Identifies one observable, undesirable consumer behavior or business outcome (e.g., website traffic increased by 140%, but subscription upgrades dropped). It strictly answers what is happening, not why.

E - EVIDENCE WHAT PROVES IT?

Documents existing factual data (traffic logs, support emails, enquiry counts, CRM records) that confirm the concern is real. Eliminates hearsay and subjective executive feelings.

G - GAP WHAT IS UNKNOWN?

Highlights critical unanswered questions that current data cannot explain (e.g., why do prospects exit after viewing the rules page? Why do users abandon checkout at the ID verification step?).

2

CONCEPT 2: UNDERSTAND THE CONSUMER — THE 5-STAGE DECISION PROCESS

Mapping Mental & Behavioral Momentum
NON-LINEAR REALITY

Why It Exists: Consumers do not make choices instantaneously. To understand why they stall or drop off, marketers map their mental and behavioral journey across five non-linear stages.

⚡ CRUCIAL RULE ON NON-LINEARITY: An incomplete form, cart abandonment, or site exit is NOT an automatic rejection! The consumer may simply need more time, want to consult a peer, wait for funding, or compare alternative providers.
STAGE 1

NEED RECOGNITION

Driven by internal stimuli (e.g., career goals, hunger) or external triggers (workplace changes, broken tools). Ad clicks here show initial curiosity, not purchase readiness!

Trigger prompts need
STAGE 2

INFORMATION SEARCH

The consumer seeks answers across digital and physical channels (search engines, course portals, peer discussions, reviews).

Channels searched for answers
STAGE 3

EVALUATION OF ALTERNATIVES

Weighing competing options using specific criteria (price, convenience, relevance, schedule fit) while seeking to minimize personal and financial risk.

Criteria used to compare
STAGE 4

DECISION

The point where the consumer acts (submits enquiry, signs up, swipes card), delays, or chooses a rival alternative.

Moment of action or delay
STAGE 5

POST-PURCHASE BEHAVIOUR

Actual experience after the decision. If case data lacks this, it is recorded as an evidence gap rather than fabricating customer satisfaction!

Experience & outcome

THE 4 PRACTICAL QUESTIONS (PROMPTED AT EVERY SINGLE STAGE)

1. PURPOSE:

What is the consumer actively trying to achieve at this exact point in time?

2. BEHAVIOUR & CHANNEL:

What do they do, where does it happen, and what role does the platform play?

3. INFLUENCE:

What personal, social, or situational factors shape and alter their choice?

4. EVIDENCE & UNCERTAINTY:

What factual data supports this finding, and what remains an unvalidated unknown?

3

CONCEPT 3: EXAMINE EVIDENCE — THE CONSUMER EVIDENCE SUMMARY (CES)

Minimum Viable Evidence Base Protocol
ANTI-ASSUMPTION SHIELD

Why It Exists: Prevents marketers from relying on "manager opinions," convenient online reviews, or AI-generated assumptions. It establishes a Minimum Viable Evidence Base—the best relevant data reasonably available to form a preliminary conclusion.

# FIELD CONCEPTUAL PURPOSE QUALITY BENCHMARK
1 1. QUESTION Formulates a focused question arising from consumer decision findings (e.g., "Why do evening applicants stall at module credit checks?"). Laser-targeted, avoids broad generalizations.
2 2. SOURCE Identifies the specific data log, survey, CRM record, user interview, or analytical report. Named empirical audit trail.
3 3. FINDING States a careful, objective conclusion without overclaiming beyond what the data proves. Strictly empirical observations only.
4 4. LIMITATION Identifies the main constraint affecting confidence (e.g., small sample size, self-selected respondents, missing motivation context). Acknowledges analytical boundaries.
5 5. STATUS Assigns a confidence status: Supported (solid evidence), Preliminary (partial evidence), or To Validate (unproven assumption). Prevents false confidence.
4

CONCEPT 4: IDENTIFY MEANINGFUL GROUPS — AUDIENCE SEGMENTATION (4 BASES)

Shatter The Demographic Trap
MULTIDIMENSIONAL PROFILING

Why It Exists: Avoids the Demographic Trap—grouping consumers solely by age, gender, or income. Generic demographic buckets describe who someone is, but almost never explain why or how they make buying decisions.

THE DEMOGRAPHIC TRAP "Prince Charles and Ozzy Osbourne share the same demographics. Do they buy the same things?"
DEMOGRAPHIC Observable Characteristics

Occupation, formal education level, household income tier, employment status.

GEOGRAPHIC Location Nuances

Physical distance from campus/store, urban vs. rural, commute realities, regional access constraints.

PSYCHOGRAPHIC Internal Psychology

Personal ambitions, fear of obsolescence, core priorities, perceived career risk, personal values.

BEHAVIOURAL Observed Actions

Search query syntax, repeated late-night module visits, syllabus downloads, review comparison habits.

Concept of Meaningful Segments:

A truly useful segment combines multiple bases (e.g., career-transition goals [Psychographic] + repeated evening curriculum page visits [Behavioural] + working mid-level corporate analyst [Demographic]) to reveal a distinct, predictable decision pattern.

5

CONCEPT 5: PRIORITISE AUDIENCE & PERSONA HANDOVER

Focus Before Execution
Why It Exists: "If you want everybody, you have nobody." To diagnose friction precisely, an organization must select ONE provisional priority audience.
3 Practical Selection Checks:
1. Connection to Concern: Does this group's specific difficulty directly drive the business problem identified in CCEG?
2. Importance of Need: Does their decision involve high personal, emotional, time, or financial commitment?
3. Strength of Evidence: Is there sufficient factual, verifiable data backing this segment's behavior?
THE PERSONA HANDOVER RULE: This is a factual document passing 6 empirical evidence fields into deeper analysis, NOT a fictional cartoon profile with made-up coffee habits or stock images!
6

CONCEPT 6: PRECISE DIAGNOSIS & PROBLEM STATEMENT

The 8-Field Journey & Synthesis
The Diagnostic Sequence:
  • Customer Journey Map (8 Fields): Journey Stage, Goal, Action, Information Need, Touchpoint, Evidence, Friction, and Limitation to isolate the Priority Journey Area.
  • Touchpoint & Competitor Diagnosis: Audit priority touchpoint against a key rival to isolate the exact friction mechanism.
  • PESTLE External Trends: Macro scan (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental) driving consumer anxiety.
THE PRELIMINARY PROBLEM STATEMENT FORMULA

"[Priority Audience] fails to [Desired Action] at [Priority Touchpoint] because they experience [Specific Friction]. While [External Trend] increases their need, [Important Uncertainty] remains."

UNIT 3: GO-TO-MARKET PERSONAS & ALGORITHMIC TARGETING

Creative Signals Over Manual Micro-Targeting
ALGORITHMIC SHIFT

GTM PERSONA VS. PROVISIONAL AUDIENCE

While Phase 1 defined a broad evidence-based segment without a face or voice, GTM Personas give marketing and creative teams semi-fictional representations (names, roles, decision triggers, and core messaging angles) so creators picture exactly who they are writing hooks and designing video for.

MODERN ALGORITHMIC TARGETING CONCEPT

Media planners no longer micromanage narrow interest clusters. Modern ad platforms (Meta Advantage+, Google Optimized Targeting) use machine learning to expand reach dynamically. The modern planner's job is feeding uncompromising creative signals, server-side Conversions API (CAPI) events, and strict audience exclusion lists.

UNIT 4: CONVERSION FUNNEL DESIGN & MEANINGFUL KPI DASHBOARD

Banishing Vanity Metrics Forever
DATA ACCOUNTABILITY

The Meaningful Metric Concept: A metric is meaningful ONLY if it is explicitly attached to a specific funnel stage and directionally indicates whether the prospect is moving closer to taking action. Page views and impressions are meaningless in isolation.

FUNNEL STAGE MEANINGFUL KPI METRIC CONCEPTUAL & DIAGNOSTIC PURPOSE
1. Need Recognition CPM / Completed Video Views (ThruPlays) Measures directional top-of-funnel reach and qualified attention without confusing passive eyeballs with buyer intent.
2. Info Search Content Engagement Rate / Scroll Depth Confirms educational content is successfully pulling traffic in and solving initial knowledge voids.
3. Evaluation Category / Curriculum Page Visits & CTR Signals active consideration and option comparison against market rivals.
4. Decision Conversion Rate (CVR) / CPA / ROAS Measures hard commercial commitments, customer acquisition costs, and immediate return on advertising spend.
5. Post-Purchase TACOS / Repeat Purchase / Referral Rate Evaluates Total Advertising Cost of Sales across full revenue, customer retention, and compounding lifetime value (LTV).

CONCEPT 1: ALEX HORMOZI'S "CATEGORY OF ONE" (GRAND SLAM OFFER)

Convert Journey Friction into High-Value Stacks
GRAND SLAM OFFER
1. SELLING IN A VACUUM

Re-positions an offering so uniquely that it cannot be directly compared to any commodity competitor. Buying decisions shift from "Your Product vs. Competitor B" into a binary choice: Your Unique Offer vs. Doing Nothing.

2. NEW OPPORTUNITY VS. IMPROVEMENT

Never sell an "improvement offer" (10% cheaper or slightly faster), which immediately triggers price resistance and past failure memories. Introduce a New Opportunity that renders old methods obsolete.

3. FRICTION-TO-VALUE STACKS

Takes every specific point of friction identified in Step 6 journey mapping and builds a direct bonus tool, template, or risk-reversal guarantee to completely eliminate it.

CONCEPT 2: PHILIP KOTLER'S MARKETING 7.0 (THE COGNITIVE COMPASS)

Engaging the Augmented Human in the Era of Digital Overload
NEURO-STRATEGY
The Target: The Augmented Human

A buyer whose thinking, feeling, evaluating, and purchasing behaviors are heavily guided by algorithmic recommendations, instant feedback loops, and chronic digital cognitive overload.

1. ATTENTION BRAIN TOP-FUNNEL
Pattern Gating & Anti-Blindness

Bypasses "banner blindness" using Lo-Fi, raw, authentic, human-first content that breaks unconscious scroll patterns without triggering ad defense mechanisms.

Tactics: Unpolished iPhone hooks, contrarian text overlays, behind-the-scenes reality.
2. SOCIAL BRAIN MID-FUNNEL
Belonging & StoryBrand (SB7)

Engages micro-communities and leverages Donald Miller's StoryBrand (SB7) narrative framework: The Customer is always the Hero with a problem; your brand is merely the Guide with a plan.

Tactics: Peer validations, cohort testimonials, community Slack access, shared identity badges.
3. REWARD BRAIN BOTTOM-FUNNEL
Dopamine & Frictionless Value

Aligns value propositions with natural cognitive trade-offs to deliver friction-free "WOW moments" at checkout and instantaneous post-purchase gratification.

Tactics: Instant unlockables, 1-click enrollments, personalized onboarding video within 60s.
